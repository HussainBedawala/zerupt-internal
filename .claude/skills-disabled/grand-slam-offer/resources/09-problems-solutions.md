# Phase 5: Problems & Solutions Brainstorm

> Source: $100M Offers, Chapters 10-11 — "Creating Your Grand Slam Offer"

## Core Principle

Your offer is NOT your product. Your offer is **the collection of solutions to every problem standing between your customer and their dream outcome.**

The more problems you solve, the more valuable the offer. The more completely you solve each problem, the more valuable the offer.

## Step 1: List EVERY Problem

Think through the entire customer journey and list every obstacle, fear, concern, and friction point:

### Before Purchase
- What's stopping them from buying?
- What have they tried before that failed?
- What are they afraid will happen (or not happen)?
- What objections do they have?
- What do they not understand?
- What do they not believe?

### During Use / Implementation
- What's hard about getting started?
- What's confusing?
- What takes too long?
- Where do they get stuck?
- What makes them want to quit?
- What's annoying or frustrating?

### After (Getting the Result)
- How do they maintain the result?
- What could cause them to lose the result?
- What's the next problem that appears after solving this one?
- How do they know it's actually working?

## Step 2: Create a Solution for EVERY Problem

For each problem identified, create a specific solution. These solutions become the **building blocks** of your offer.

Format: **Problem → Solution**

Example (for a SaaS onboarding tool):
| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Don't know where to start | Step-by-step onboarding wizard |
| Data migration is painful | White-glove data migration service |
| Team won't adopt it | Team training workshop |
| Afraid it won't work for their industry | Industry-specific templates |
| Takes too long to see results | Quick-start preset configurations |
| Don't trust it yet | 30-day full money-back guarantee |
| Scared of being locked in | Easy export + month-to-month pricing |

## The Exhaustive Approach

Hormozi emphasizes: **be EXHAUSTIVE.** Don't stop at 5-10 problems. Push to 20, 30, even 40+. The more problems you identify, the more complete your offer becomes.

Think about problems at every level:
- **Practical** problems (how do I do this?)
- **Emotional** problems (what if I fail? what if it's a waste?)
- **Social** problems (what will my team/boss/customers think?)
- **Technical** problems (will this work with my existing systems?)

## Interview Goal

By the end of this phase, you should have:
- A comprehensive list of 15-30+ problems across before/during/after
- A specific solution for each problem
- Raw material for building the offer stack in the next phase
