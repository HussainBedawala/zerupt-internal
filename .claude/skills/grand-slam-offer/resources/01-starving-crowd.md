# Phase 1: Finding the Starving Crowd

> Source: $100M Offers, Chapters 5-6 — "Finding the Right Market" / "The Starving Crowd"

## Core Principle

The #1 factor in offer success is NOT a better product — it's a **starving crowd**. A mediocre offer to a desperate market beats an amazing offer to a lukewarm one.

Hormozi's hamburger stand analogy: If you could have only ONE advantage over every competing hamburger stand, what would it be? Not better meat, not a better location, not lower prices — **a starving crowd.**

## The 3 Market Selection Criteria

Rate each market on a 1-10 scale:

### 1. Massive Pain (Do they DESPERATELY need this?)
- Are they actively searching for a solution?
- Is the pain acute (urgent) or chronic (tolerable)?
- Are they already spending money trying to solve this?
- Would they describe themselves as "desperate" or just "interested"?

### 2. Purchasing Power (Can they afford to pay?)
- Does this market have money or access to money?
- Are they already spending on solutions in this category?
- Is the budget holder the same as the user? If not, who decides?
- Can they afford a premium price (not just a cheap solution)?

### 3. Easy to Target (Can you find and reach them?)
- Do they congregate somewhere (online communities, events, associations)?
- Are there existing lists, directories, or channels to reach them?
- Can you identify them with specific, observable characteristics?
- Is there a way to get in front of them without massive ad spend?

## Growing vs. Shrinking Markets

- **Always pick a growing market.** Growing markets forgive mistakes. Shrinking markets punish even great execution.
- A rising tide lifts all boats. You want the tailwind.
- Signs of a growing market: increasing search volume, new entrants, expanding budgets, media attention, regulatory activity.

## The 4 Market Indicators (from Hormozi)

A great market has ALL of these:
1. **Massive pain** — they need it solved NOW
2. **Purchasing power** — they can pay premium prices
3. **Easy to target** — you can find them efficiently
4. **Growing** — the market is expanding, not contracting

## Interview Goal

By the end of this phase, you should have:
- The specific market the founder is targeting (or should target)
- A 1-10 score on each of the 3 criteria
- Confidence that this market can support a Grand Slam Offer
- If the market scores low, a discussion about pivoting to a better one
