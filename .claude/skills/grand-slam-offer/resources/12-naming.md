# Phase 9: Naming Your Offer

> Source: $100M Offers, Chapter 15 — "Naming Your Offer"

## Core Principle

The name of your offer is the first thing prospects see. A great name does three things:
1. **Grabs attention** — stops the scroll, makes them read more
2. **Communicates value** — they instantly understand what they get
3. **Creates desire** — they WANT it before they even know the details

## The MAGIC Formula

Hormozi's naming framework:

### M — Make a Compelling Reason WHY
- Why does this offer exist?
- Why now?
- Why should they care?
- This is the hook that creates context

### A — Announce Your AVATAR
- Who is this specifically for?
- The more specific, the more the right person feels "this is for ME"
- Use their language, their identity, their role

### G — Give Them a GOAL
- What will they achieve?
- State the dream outcome in the name
- Make it specific and desirable

### I — Indicate a TIME FRAME
- How fast will they get the result?
- A specific time frame increases urgency and believability
- "In 30 days" "In 6 weeks" "This quarter"

### C — Complete With a CONTAINER WORD
- What IS the thing?
- Container words: Challenge, Blueprint, System, Accelerator, Academy, Bootcamp, Program, Toolkit, Formula, Framework, Playbook, Masterclass, Sprint, Intensive
- The container word sets expectations for the format and experience

## MAGIC Formula Examples

| Component | Example 1 | Example 2 |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **M** (Reason) | "Launch Special" | "2026 Edition" |
| **A** (Avatar) | "for B2B SaaS Founders" | "for E-commerce Store Owners" |
| **G** (Goal) | "Get to $1M ARR" | "Double Your Conversion Rate" |
| **I** (Time) | "in 90 Days" | "in 6 Weeks" |
| **C** (Container) | "Accelerator" | "Intensive" |
| **Full Name** | "Launch Special: The $1M ARR Accelerator for B2B SaaS Founders — 90 Days" | "The 6-Week Conversion Doubler Intensive for E-commerce Store Owners (2026 Edition)" |

## Naming Tips

- **Test multiple names** — the name can make or break the offer
- **Use numbers** — specific numbers are more believable ("$1M" not "lots of money")
- **Avoid jargon** — use words your CUSTOMER uses, not industry insider terms
- **Make it say-able** — if someone can't tell a friend about it easily, simplify it
- **Short beats long** — but clarity beats brevity. If you need more words to be clear, use them.

## For SaaS Specifically

SaaS offers often name:
- **The product tier**: "Growth Plan", "Scale Plan", "Enterprise Plan"
- **The onboarding package**: "Quick Launch Setup", "White-Glove Migration"
- **The bonus bundle**: "The [Industry] Success Kit"
- **The overall offer**: Combines product + services + bonuses under one name

## Interview Goal

By the end of this phase, you should have:
- A name built using the MAGIC formula
- Each component explicitly defined (M, A, G, I, C)
- A tagline or one-liner that communicates the offer's value
- The name tested against: "Would the ideal customer stop scrolling for this?"
