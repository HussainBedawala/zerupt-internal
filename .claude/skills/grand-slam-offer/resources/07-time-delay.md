# Value Equation Deep-Dive: Time Delay

> Source: $100M Offers, Chapter 9 — "The Value Equation" (Denominator, Variable 3)

## Core Principle

Time Delay = the time between when someone purchases and when they receive the promised result.

**The shorter the time delay, the more valuable the offer.**

People pay massive premiums for speed:
- Overnight shipping costs 10x+ more than standard
- Express lanes charge more at the DMV
- "Done today" beats "done in 6 weeks" every time

## Two Types of Time

### 1. Time to First Value (Quick Win)
- How quickly can the customer experience SOME result?
- This is critical for retention and satisfaction
- Even a small win early builds momentum and belief

### 2. Time to Full Result (Dream Outcome)
- How long until they achieve the complete dream outcome?
- This can be longer IF quick wins are delivered along the way

## The Strategy: Quick Wins + Long-Term Results

The best offers deliver:
1. **Immediate** — something happens right away (access, first setup, first insight)
2. **Short-term** — meaningful progress within days/weeks (first result, first milestone)
3. **Long-term** — the full dream outcome over time (full transformation)

This gives the customer confidence at every stage.

## How to Minimize Time Delay

1. **Streamline onboarding** — get them to value faster
2. **Remove steps** — what can you skip or do for them?
3. **Front-load wins** — design the experience to deliver results early
4. **Automate** — technology can compress time dramatically
5. **Done-for-you setup** — don't make them figure it out

## For SaaS Specifically

- Onboarding wizard that gets them live in minutes, not days
- Pre-built templates so they don't start from zero
- Data import that actually works
- Quick-start guides vs. lengthy documentation
- "Time to value" is the #1 SaaS retention metric

## Interview Questions for This Phase

1. How long does it currently take for a customer to see their FIRST result?
2. How long until they achieve the full dream outcome?
3. What steps slow the customer down the most?
4. What could you do to deliver a "quick win" in the first 24-48 hours?
5. What parts of the process could be automated or done-for-them?
