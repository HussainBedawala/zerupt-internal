# Phase 3: Pricing

> Source: $100M Offers, Chapter 8 — "Charging What It's Worth"

## Core Principle

**"Price is what you pay, value is what you get."** — Warren Buffett (quoted by Hormozi)

Most founders underprice because they're scared of rejection. Hormozi argues that **undercharging is a disservice** — to you AND your customer.

## The Virtuous Cycle of Premium Pricing

```
Charge more → Attract better clients → Deliver better results
     ↑                                        ↓
More profit ← Better testimonials ← Clients more invested
```

When you charge premium:
1. **Clients are more invested** — they do the work, they get results
2. **You can invest more in delivery** — better support, better product, better experience
3. **You attract serious buyers** — not tire-kickers and complainers
4. **Your margins fund growth** — you can hire, market, and improve
5. **Better results = better proof** — which lets you charge even more

## The Opposite: The Race to the Bottom

```
Charge less → Attract worse clients → Deliver worse results
     ↑                                        ↓
Less profit ← Worse testimonials ← Clients not invested
```

Cheap clients:
- Complain the most
- Get the worst results (no skin in the game)
- Leave the worst reviews
- Drain your energy and resources

## Commodity vs. Differentiated

- **Commodity**: interchangeable, compete on price, race to the bottom
- **Grand Slam Offer**: category of one, compete on value, charge premium

You escape commodity pricing by creating an offer so different and valuable that there's nothing to compare it to. **You can't compare apples to oranges** — that's the goal.

## Price-to-Value Discrepancy

The magic ratio: charge a **premium price** but deliver **10x the value of the price.**

- If you charge $10,000, the perceived + actual value should be $100,000+
- This isn't about being cheap — it's about being so valuable that the price feels like a steal
- The wider the gap between price and value, the easier the sale

## How to Think About Your Price

1. What is the dream outcome WORTH to the customer? (in revenue, time saved, pain avoided)
2. What are they currently spending (in money, time, and opportunity cost) trying to solve this?
3. If your solution works, what's the ROI?
4. Price should be a fraction of the value delivered

## Interview Goal

By the end of this phase, you should have:
- What they currently charge (or plan to charge)
- What the dream outcome is worth to their customer (in dollars)
- Whether their current pricing is commodity or differentiated
- A preliminary price point that follows the 10x value rule
