---
  Audit Report: Merpec ERP — Accounting Module

  Date: 27 February 2026
  Auditor: Retail ERP Accounting Expert
  Scope: Accounting module spec (product/sections/accounting/spec.md), TypeScript data types (types.ts), all UI components, cross-referenced against Sales,
   Purchase, POS, and Inventory module specs. Evaluated against IFRS standards, GCC/Kuwait tax requirements, and retail accounting best practices.
  Overall Risk Level: 🔴 Critical — Multiple gaps that will produce incorrect financial statements, prevent multi-currency operation, and create compliance
   exposure as soon as the product expands beyond Kuwait.

  ---
  Executive Summary

  Merpec's accounting module has strong UI bones — the journal entry form enforces double-entry balance, cheque lifecycle management is well-designed,
  period locking is properly two-tiered (soft/hard), and the AP/AR aging structure is sound. The team has clearly thought about what retail accountants
  actually use daily.

  However, the audit reveals five critical architectural gaps that cannot be fixed with UI polish — they require changes to the data model and backend
  design before go-live: (1) no tax configuration model exists anywhere in the system, making the VAT return module a reporting shell with no underlying
  calculation engine; (2) the entire system lacks a multi-currency layer on the accounting data types — journal entries, cheques, and vouchers have no
  currency/exchange rate fields; (3) there is no COGS journal entry logic defined for inventory sales, meaning every sale will overstate net income; (4)
  year-end closing entries (net income → retained earnings) are not designed; and (5) the cash flow statement is described as being "derived from journal
  entries" with no methodology defined — this will produce incorrect statements.

  Additionally, the product's positioning in Kuwait — where VAT does not yet exist — creates a product logic problem: the VAT module is built as a live
  operational tool (with "Mark as Filed" workflow) rather than a readiness/configuration tool. This will confuse Kuwaiti customers and must be addressed
  before launch.

  ---
  Module-by-Module Findings

  ---
  1. Chart of Accounts

  Status: ⚠️  Issues Found

  What's working well:
  - Multi-level hierarchy (up to 5 levels) with parent/child relationships
  - Five account types (Asset, Liability, Equity, Income, Expense) — correctly classified
  - Bilingual names (English + Arabic) — essential for GCC market
  - isControlAccount flag for sub-ledger accounts (AR, AP, bank)
  - Accounts with transactions can only be deactivated, not deleted — correct

  Issues found:

  #: 1
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No "Cost of Sales" account sub-type. COGS accounts are classified as plain Expense — this means the P&L cannot auto-produce a Gross Profit line
    without custom configuration
  Impact: P&L will show a flat expense list; Gross Profit calculation will require manual chart setup that every new customer must configure correctly
  Recommended Fix: Add subType field to Account: 'CostOfSales' | 'OperatingExpense' | 'FinanceCharge' | null. Use this to auto-structure the P&L statement.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 2
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No currencyCode field on Account. In a multi-currency system, individual bank/cash accounts are denominated in a specific currency (e.g., a USD
    account). Without this, bank account balances cannot be properly reported in both transaction and functional currency.
  Impact: Bank balances in foreign currencies will be shown in KWD equivalent only — no traceability to the foreign currency ledger balance
  Recommended Fix: Add currencyCode: string | null to Account. Null = functional currency. Non-null = foreign currency account.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 3
  Severity: 🟢 Low
  Issue: No normalBalance field ('Debit' or 'Credit'). This is used by the GL engine to determine if a balance is a positive or negative movement, and for
    displaying balances correctly (e.g., a liability with a debit balance is unusual and should be flagged).
  Impact: Manual journal entry errors harder to catch; unusual balances won't be flagged automatically
  Recommended Fix: Add normalBalance: 'Debit' | 'Credit' — derivable from AccountType but explicit is better for engine clarity
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 4
  Severity: 🟢 Low
  Issue: No openingBalance / openingBalanceDate fields. New customers migrating from legacy systems must enter opening balances, but there is no mechanism
    defined for this.
  Impact: Every new Merpec customer will have a zero balance sheet on day one, making the system useless for reporting until enough history accumulates
  Recommended Fix: Add opening balance support: either via a special "Opening Balance" journal entry type, or as dedicated fields on Account with a cutoff
    date

  Recommended journal entries:
  Event: Goods received and sold (COGS recognition)
    DR  Cost of Goods Sold (5xxx - Cost of Sales subtype)   [cost_amount]
    CR  Inventory                                            [cost_amount]

  This entry must auto-generate when a Sales Invoice is confirmed.
  Currently: no COGS entry is defined anywhere in the system.

  2. Journal Entries

  Status: ⚠️  Issues Found

  What's working well:
  - Double-entry enforcement (form cannot submit unless debits = credits)
  - Draft → Posted workflow
  - Reversal creates a new counter-entry (not a deletion) — audit-safe
  - Source document linkage (sourceType, sourceId, sourceLabel) — excellent
  - Auto vs manual entry distinction
  - Bulk post capability

  Issues found:

  #: 1
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: JournalEntry and JournalEntryLine have no currency or exchange rate fields. The entire type structure is single-currency. A foreign currency
    transaction (e.g., a purchase invoice in USD) cannot be properly recorded.
  Impact: Multi-currency transactions will be recorded only in KWD equivalent — the foreign currency amount is lost. Exchange rate gains/losses cannot be
    calculated or journalized. Month-end revaluation of foreign currency balances is impossible. This blocks any customer doing business in multiple
    currencies.
  Recommended Fix: Add currency: string and exchangeRate: number to JournalEntry. Add amountFC: number (foreign currency amount) and currencyFC: string to
    JournalEntryLine.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 2
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: No year-end closing entry mechanism. When a fiscal year is "closed," the system hard-locks periods but does not define closing journal entries
  that transfer net income/loss to Retained Earnings.
  Impact: At year-end, Income and Expense account balances remain on the P&L indefinitely. The balance sheet's Equity section will not reflect current-year profit correctly unless a closing entry is generated.
  Recommended Fix: Define an auto-closing entry triggered by onCloseYear(): DR All Income Accounts / CR All Expense Accounts / CR (or DR) Retained Earnings
   
    — Current Year. Add closingJournalEntryId to FiscalPeriod.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 3
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: The journal entry form has no period validation at the UI level. A user can enter any date — including dates in locked or future periods — without

    warning. The spec mentions this should be blocked, but the form component (JournalEntryForm.tsx) has no period-awareness logic.
  Impact: Users can inadvertently post entries to closed periods (backend must block, but UI gives no feedback before submission). Future-dated entries can

    distort current-period reporting.
  Recommended Fix: JournalEntryForm must receive fiscalPeriods as a prop and validate the entered date: warn on soft-locked, block on hard-locked. Show the

    period status inline next to the date field.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 4
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No reversalOf link on the JournalEntry type. When a reversing entry is created, there is no field pointing back to the original entry, and vice
    versa.
  Impact: The audit trail for reversed entries is incomplete. "Why does this entry exist?" cannot be answered from the data alone.
  Recommended Fix: Add reversalOfEntryId: string | null and reversedByEntryId: string | null to JournalEntry.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 5
  Severity: 🟢 Low
  Issue: The form enforces isBalanced = Math.abs(totalDebit - totalCredit) < 0.001. For KWD (3 decimal places), this tolerance may allow a 0.001 KWD
    imbalance to slip through. For high-value transactions, this could accumulate.
  Impact: Over time, small rounding imbalances will cause the Trial Balance to be out of balance, eroding trust in financial reports.
  Recommended Fix: Use exact decimal comparison with currency-aware rounding. For KWD, round all amounts to 3 decimal places before comparison. Consider
    using a BigDecimal library.

  3. Cheques

  Status: ⚠️  Issues Found

  What's working well:
  - Full lifecycle: Received → Issued → Deposited → Cleared → Bounced → Reissued → Cancelled
  - Status history timeline with user and notes — excellent audit trail
  - replacesId link for reissued cheques — traceability
  - Kanban view for workflow management

  Issues found:

  #: 1
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: The Cheque type has no currency field. All cheques are implicitly in the functional currency (KWD). In practice, Kuwaiti businesses regularly
    receive and issue cheques in USD, EUR, etc.
  Impact: Foreign currency cheques cannot be tracked. The bank account they clear to will have an incorrect balance if the cheque's currency differs from
    the account's currency.
  Recommended Fix: Add currency: string and exchangeRate: number to Cheque.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 2
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: No journal entries are defined for cheque status transitions, despite the spec stating "each transition auto-generates the appropriate journal
    entry." The types.ts has no journalEntryId link on Cheque or ChequeStatusHistoryEntry.
  Impact: Cheque lifecycle is tracked visually but has no financial impact on the books. A bounced cheque will not reverse the cash/AR entry. A cleared
    cheque will not debit the bank account. The accounting will be completely disconnected from cheque reality.
  Recommended Fix: Define and implement all cheque journal entries (see below). Add journalEntryId: string | null to ChequeStatusHistoryEntry.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 3
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No bounced cheque bank charge handling. When a cheque bounces, the customer's bank typically charges a fee (10–50 KWD). This fee is a cost to the
    business and must be recorded.
  Impact: Bank fees from bounced cheques will not appear in the books, understating bank charges expense.
  Recommended Fix: Add bounceFee: number and bounceFeeAccountId: string fields to the bounce status transition.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 4
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No due-date maturity alert. Post-dated cheques that are approaching their due date should trigger a notification. There is no notification logic
    described.
  Impact: Finance team will miss cheque due dates manually — especially critical for issued cheques (you must have cash in the bank before the cheque
    clears).
  Recommended Fix: Add a scheduled notification: "Cheque #XXXX due in 3 days — Amount: KWD 5,000. Ensure sufficient balance in [Bank Account]."

  Recommended journal entries for all cheque transitions:
  Event: Customer cheque received (Received status)
    DR  Cheques in Hand (Current Asset)     [amount]
    CR  Accounts Receivable — Customer      [amount]

  Event: Received cheque deposited to bank (Deposited status)
    DR  Bank — Cheques in Transit           [amount]
    CR  Cheques in Hand                     [amount]

  Event: Cheque cleared by bank (Cleared status)
    DR  Bank Account                        [amount]
    CR  Bank — Cheques in Transit           [amount]

  Event: Cheque bounced (Bounced status)
    DR  Accounts Receivable — Customer      [amount]   (re-opens the receivable)
    DR  Bank Charges Expense                [fee]      (if bounce fee charged)
    CR  Bank — Cheques in Transit           [amount]
    CR  Bank Account                        [fee]      (bank debits our account for fee)

  Event: Issued cheque (Issued status — paying a supplier)
    DR  Accounts Payable — Supplier         [amount]
    CR  Cheques Issued — Outstanding        [amount]   (a liability until cleared)

  Event: Issued cheque cleared by bank (Cleared status)
    DR  Cheques Issued — Outstanding        [amount]
    CR  Bank Account                        [amount]

  4. Receipt Vouchers

  Status: ⚠️  Issues Found

  What's working well:
  - Allocation to specific invoices (oldest-first or manual) — correct AR management
  - Payment method breakdown (Cash, Bank Transfer, Cheque)
  - Draft → Posted workflow with auto journal entry on posting
  - Link back to cheque record when payment method is cheque

  Issues found:

  #: 1
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: ReceiptVoucher has no currency or exchangeRate field. If a customer pays in USD (common for import/export businesses), the system cannot record
  the
     transaction currency and will only capture the KWD-equivalent amount at the time of entry.
  Impact: Foreign currency receipts will be booked incorrectly. Exchange rate differences between invoice date and payment date (which must be recognized
  as
    FX gain/loss) cannot be calculated.
  Recommended Fix: Add currency: string, exchangeRate: number, amountFC: number to ReceiptVoucher.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 2
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: No "advance payment" handling. If a customer pays before any invoice exists (common in retail B2B), the receipt voucher requires allocations — but

    there is no mechanism to record an unallocated advance. The allocations array must contain at least one invoice reference per the current type.
  Impact: Prepayments from customers cannot be recorded. Finance teams will either leave cash floating, or create dummy invoices — both are accounting
    violations.
  Recommended Fix: Allow allocations to be empty (advance payment). Advance payments should credit Customer Deposits — Liability not Accounts Receivable.
    Add isAdvancePayment: boolean and a mechanism to later allocate against invoices.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 3
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: Overpayment handling undefined. If a customer pays KWD 1,000 against a KWD 800 invoice, the KWD 200 excess is undefined. Should it create a credit

    balance on the AR account, or be held as a customer deposit?
  Impact: Overpayments will either be lost in the AR balance (hard to identify later) or will leave the journal entry unbalanced.
  Recommended Fix: Specify overpayment behavior: either create a Customer Deposit liability for the excess, or allow a negative allocation line on a future

    invoice. Show the overpayment amount prominently in the voucher UI.

  Recommended journal entry (Receipt Voucher posting):
  Event: Customer payment received via bank transfer (foreign currency)
    DR  Bank Account — USD (at exchange rate)          KWD 750.000  (= USD 2,500 × 0.300)
    CR  Accounts Receivable — Customer                 KWD 740.000  (invoice amount at original rate)
    CR  FX Gain — Exchange Rate Differences            KWD  10.000  (rate moved favorably)

  Currently: no currency/exchange rate fields exist to perform this calculation.

  5. Payment Vouchers

  Status: ⚠️  Issues Found

  Same structural issues as Receipt Vouchers (no currency/exchange rate fields, no advance payment handling for deposits paid to suppliers). Additionally:

  #: 1
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: No currency/exchange rate on PaymentVoucher (same as ReceiptVoucher)
  Impact: Foreign currency supplier payments cannot be properly recorded
  Recommended Fix: Same fix as Receipt Voucher
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 2
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No partial payment with discount handling. In Kuwait and GCC, suppliers often grant an early payment discount (e.g., "2% if paid within 10 days").

    The allocation model has no discountAmount field.
  Impact: Settlement discounts will either be ignored (overpayment of the supplier), or recorded as a manual journal entry with no link to the payment.
  Recommended Fix: Add discountAmount: number and discountAccountId: string to VoucherAllocation. Journal entry: DR AP [full invoice amount] / CR Bank [net
   
    paid] / CR Purchase Discount Income [discount]

  6. Accounts Receivable / Payable

  Status: ✅ Sound (UI) / ⚠️  Issues in underlying model

  What's working well:
  - Aging buckets (Current, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90, 91-120, 120+) — industry standard
  - Drill-down to individual invoices
  - Last payment date tracking
  - Export to PDF/Excel

  Issues found:

  #: 1
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: Aging calculated at what date? The CustomerAging and SupplierAging types have bucket amounts but no asOfDate field. Without knowing the snapshot
    date, the aging report is meaningless — a "30-day" bucket could mean different things depending on when the report was run.
  Impact: Finance teams cannot use aging reports for audit or credit decisions without knowing the "as of" date. Historical aging snapshots (needed for
    auditors) are impossible.
  Recommended Fix: Add asOfDate: string to the aging data structure. Allow users to run aging as of any date, not just today.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 2
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No credit limit breach journal entry or hold mechanism. The Sales spec mentions credit limit warnings, but there's no link between the AR balance
    and the credit limit field at the accounting level.
  Impact: A customer can exceed their credit limit and continue to receive goods. The accounting module cannot flag customers in breach.
  Recommended Fix: Surface creditLimitUsed vs creditLimit in the AR aging view. Flag customers exceeding their limit with a "Credit Hold" indicator.

  7. Bank & Cash

  Status: ⚠️  Issues Found

  What's working well:
  - Multiple bank/cash accounts with type distinction
  - IBAN field — correct for GCC banking
  - lastReconciledDate tracking
  - Fund transfer between accounts

  Issues found:

  #: 1
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: Bank reconciliation has no data model. The spec describes a reconciliation tool (match system transactions to bank statement entries) but the
    BankAccount type only has lastReconciledDate — there is no BankStatement, BankStatementLine, or ReconciliationMatch type.
  Impact: The reconciliation UI described in the spec cannot be built without a data model. The "mark as reconciled" callback (onReconcileTransaction)
  takes
    a transactionId but there is no reconciliation transaction type defined.
  Recommended Fix: Define: BankStatement { id, bankAccountId, statementDate, openingBalance, closingBalance, lines[] }, BankStatementLine { id, date,
    description, amount, reconciledTransactionId }, ReconciliationStatus: 'Unmatched' | 'Matched' | 'Cleared'
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 2
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: Fund transfer journal entry undefined. onTransferFunds() takes from/to account IDs and amount, but there is no definition of the resulting journal

    entry, nor any handling of bank transfer fees (common in GCC interbank transfers).
  Impact: Transfers will move money between bank accounts on the balance sheet, but if the fee isn't captured, the bank account balance will not reconcile
    to the actual bank statement.
  Recommended Fix: Define transfer journal entry: DR Target Bank Account [amount] / CR Source Bank Account [amount]. Add transferFee: number and
    feeAccountId: string to the transfer form.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 3
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: BankAccount has no overdraftLimit field. In Kuwait, businesses frequently use overdraft facilities. An overdraft should show as a current
    liability, not a negative asset.
  Impact: A bank account in overdraft will show as a negative balance under Assets — technically incorrect; should appear as a separate liability.
  Recommended Fix: Add overdraftLimit: number to BankAccount. When balance < 0, classify as "Bank Overdraft" under Current Liabilities in the Balance
  Sheet.

  8. VAT Returns

  Status: ⚠️  Issues Found — and a fundamental market-fit issue

  What's working well:
  - Output/Input VAT breakdown by tax code
  - Net VAT due calculation
  - Filing status workflow (Draft → Filed with reference number)
  - Export to PDF and Excel

  Issues found:

  #: 1
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: Kuwait has no VAT. The primary launch market is Kuwait, where VAT has not been implemented. The VAT Returns module currently shows a live
    operational workflow (with "Mark as Filed" button, tax authority reference fields, KWD amounts). Running this in Kuwait will confuse every customer.
  Impact: Finance teams in Kuwait will see a module that doesn't apply to their business. Worse, if the system silently calculates "0% VAT" on all
    transactions, customers expanding to UAE/KSA will inherit incorrect configurations.
  Recommended Fix: For Kuwait: replace the VAT module with a "VAT Readiness" setup panel — show the chart of accounts has VAT accounts pre-configured,
  allow
    configuration of tax codes (rates, exemptions), and clearly label it "Not yet active — Kuwait VAT not yet in effect."
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 2
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: No Tax Configuration model exists. Neither the spec nor types.ts defines TaxCode, TaxGroup, or TaxRate. The roadmap mentions "VAT configuration
  and
    returns — tax groups, tax codes, VAT return report" but these types do not exist. The VatReturnLine.taxCode references a tax code, but there is nowhere

    to define what that code means, its rate, whether it's standard/zero/exempt, or which jurisdiction it applies to.
  Impact: The VAT calculation engine has no configuration to drive it. Tax amounts will either be hardcoded (wrong) or uncomputable. The system cannot
    correctly handle standard, zero-rated, and exempt supplies simultaneously.
  Recommended Fix: Add to types.ts: TaxCode { id, code, name, rate, type: 'Standard' | 'ZeroRated' | 'Exempt' | 'ReverseCharge', jurisdiction,
    effectiveFrom, effectiveTo }. Tax codes must be assignable per product, per customer, and per supplier.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 3
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: VatReturnLine has no rate field. The line shows taxableAmount and vatAmount but not the rate. A tax auditor reviewing the return cannot verify
  that
    the correct rate was applied to each supply.
  Impact: Tax authority audits will require reconciliation between taxable amounts and VAT amounts — if the rate isn't stored, this reconciliation is
  manual
    and error-prone.
  Recommended Fix: Add rate: number to VatReturnLine.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 4
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No credit note treatment in VAT return. Credit notes reduce output VAT for the period. The VatReturnSection shows sales data but there is no
    separate credit note section (required by most GCC VAT forms, which have separate boxes for adjustments).
  Impact: VAT return will overstate output VAT if credit notes are not separately identified. Tax authority returns in UAE/KSA have separate line items for

    credit notes and adjustments.
  Recommended Fix: Add creditNoteVat: VatReturnSection to VatReturn. Show credit notes as a reduction from output VAT, not netted into the taxable amount.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 5
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No VAT period selector/generator. The UI receives vatReturn: VatReturn | null as a prop — there is no mechanism for a user to generate a VAT
  return
    for a specific period, or to list previous periods.
  Impact: Users cannot navigate to previous VAT periods or generate returns for a new period. The VAT return is passive (display only) rather than active
    (compute and generate).
  Recommended Fix: Add vatReturnPeriods: VatReturn[] prop and a period selector. Add an onGenerateVatReturn(periodStart, periodEnd): void callback that
    triggers the backend to calculate the return for a specified period.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 6
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: KSA ZATCA e-invoicing not in Phase 1 scope, but the tech architecture must support it. The QR code requirement, cryptographic stamp, XML format,
    and API integration with ZATCA are non-trivial. Phase 2 says "KSA expansion" but ZATCA compliance takes 3–6 months to implement properly. Starting
  early
     is critical.
  Impact: Going live in KSA without ZATCA Phase 2 compliance is illegal and carries penalties of SAR 1,000 per non-compliant invoice.
  Recommended Fix: Flag ZATCA as a Phase 1 architecture concern even if not user-facing: invoice data model must include UUID, the system must support QR
    code generation, and invoice numbering must be sequential with no gaps.

  9. Financial Statements

  Status: ⚠️  Issues Found

  What's working well:
  - All five standard statements: P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, Trial Balance, General Ledger
  - Comparison mode (current vs prior period with variance %)
  - Branch-level and consolidated views
  - Drill-down to journal entry lines
  - Export to PDF/Excel

  Issues found:

  #: 1
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: Cash Flow Statement methodology undefined. The spec says it is "derived from journal entries" but does not define the method (direct or indirect).

    The indirect method (most common under IFRS) starts with net profit and adjusts for non-cash items and working capital changes — this requires
    classifying every account as operating/investing/financing. No such classification exists in the Account type.
  Impact: Without a cash flow classification on accounts, the system cannot generate a correct cash flow statement. An incorrect cash flow statement is
    worse than none — it misleads management about the company's liquidity.
  Recommended Fix: Add cashFlowCategory: 'Operating' | 'Investing' | 'Financing' | 'None' | null to Account. The Cash Flow Statement engine reads this to
    classify movements. Alternatively, use the direct method: classify all bank/cash movements by transaction type.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 2
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: COGS does not exist in the system. The P&L requires: Revenue → Gross Profit (Revenue − COGS) → Operating Profit (Gross Profit − Opex). But there
  is
     no mechanism that auto-generates COGS entries when inventory is sold. The system will show revenue on every sale but zero COGS, producing an inflated
    gross margin and incorrect net profit.
  Impact: Every financial statement will show incorrect profitability. Customer CFOs will immediately notice that COGS is missing. The product will fail
    accounting review on day one.
  Recommended Fix: Define COGS journal entry trigger: when a Sales Invoice is confirmed, DR COGS [weighted average cost] / CR Inventory [same amount]. This

    must link to the inventory valuation engine (weighted average cost or FIFO, as specified in the Inventory module).
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 3
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No inter-period comparison for Balance Sheet. Balance Sheet comparisons require prior year closing balances. Since there is no year-end closing
    entry mechanism (see Journal Entries issue #2), the "comparison mode" for Balance Sheet will show incorrect prior year figures.
  Impact: Balance Sheet comparison will be meaningless until year-end closing is implemented.
  Recommended Fix: Block or clearly warn when balance sheet comparison is selected and no prior year closing has been performed.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 4
  Severity: 🟢 Low
  Issue: No notes to financial statements. Professional financial statements require disclosure notes (accounting policies, depreciation rates, related
    party transactions). While this may be out of scope for Phase 1, the export format should have a placeholder.
  Impact: Auditors and banks will ask for notes. Without them, the exported statements are not audit-ready.
  Recommended Fix: Add a configurable "Notes" section to the Financial Statements export. Can be free-text in Phase 1.

  10. Period Closing

  Status: ⚠️  Issues Found

  What's working well:
  - Three-tier lock model (Open → Soft Locked → Hard Locked) — well-designed
  - Admin-only unlock for hard-locked periods
  - Annual close triggers batch hard-lock of all 12 months
  - Confirmation dialogs before irreversible actions

  Issues found:

  #: 1
  Severity: 🔴 Critical
  Issue: No year-end closing entries. onCloseYear() hard-locks all months but does not trigger closing journal entries (transfer Income/Expense balances to

    Retained Earnings). Without closing entries, Income and Expense accounts carry forward into the next year — the P&L for Year 2 will include Year 1
    balances.
  Impact: Financial statements for any year after Year 1 will be incorrect. The Balance Sheet's Retained Earnings figure will not update. This is a
    fundamental accounting violation.
  Recommended Fix: onCloseYear() must: (1) sum all Income account balances, (2) sum all Expense account balances, (3) generate a closing journal entry DR
    all Income accounts / CR all Expense accounts / CR or DR Retained Earnings for the difference, (4) hard-lock all periods.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 2
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: FiscalPeriod has no fiscalYearStart configuration. The system assumes a January–December calendar year. Many GCC businesses use a different fiscal

    year (April–March, July–June, or a Hijri-calendar-based year).
  Impact: Businesses with non-calendar fiscal years cannot use the period closing module correctly. Regulatory reporting in some GCC countries requires
    fiscal years aligned with specific dates.
  Recommended Fix: Add a FiscalYear entity: { id, startMonth: number, startYear: number, endMonth: number, endYear: number, label }. FiscalPeriods should
    reference a FiscalYear.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 3
  Severity: 🟡 Medium
  Issue: No pre-closing checklist enforcement. The spec mentions a "closing checklist (all months reconciled, all bank accounts reconciled, VAT returns
    filed, etc.)" but the component has no checklist logic — the "Close Year" button is available regardless of reconciliation status.
  Impact: Users can close a year with unreconciled bank accounts or unfiled VAT returns, creating compliance risk and requiring re-opening of locked
    periods.
  Recommended Fix: Before onCloseYear() fires, show a mandatory checklist: (1) All bank accounts reconciled? (2) VAT return filed for all applicable
    periods? (3) All AR/AP balances reviewed? Make items advisory but show clearly which are incomplete.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  #: 4
  Severity: 🟢 Low
  Issue: The FiscalPeriod type has lockedBy and lockedAt but no unlockedBy / unlockedAt audit trail. Hard-locked period unlocks are a high-risk action —
    full audit trail is required.
  Impact: Cannot prove who unlocked a period if accounts are investigated.
  Recommended Fix: Add lastUnlockedBy: string | null and lastUnlockedAt: string | null to FiscalPeriod.

  ---
  The most important single message from this audit: the product has a strong accounting UI framework, but the data model does not yet support the
  accounting engine that needs to sit behind it. The UI components are ready to connect to an accounting engine — but that engine does not yet exist in a
  complete form. The P0 items above are the specification for what that engine must do before a single live customer transaction is processed.
